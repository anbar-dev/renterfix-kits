Quick verdict

A good move-out clean is a sequence, not a shopping spree. Start with photos and a copy of the move-out instructions, then work from empty surfaces to floors. Focus on grease, hair, dust, soap buildup, appliance interiors, and visible scuffs. Do not chase normal wear with a harsh product that damages paint, flooring, or fixtures.

The minimum move-out cleaning kit

Buy the smallest set that covers the surfaces you actually have. Most renters need reusable cloths, a floor tool, one targeted scrubber, one residue cleaner, bathroom basics, and gloves. Add specialty products only after the final inspection notes tell you they are necessary.

Clean in this order

Order Area What to do
1 Photos and notes Photograph each room, appliance, wall, floor, and existing mark before the final clean.
2 Kitchen Empty cabinets, clean cabinet fronts, degrease the stovetop, wipe the fridge, sink, handles, and backsplash.
3 Bathroom Clean toilet, sink, tub or shower, mirror, fixtures, vanity, vents, and the floor around the toilet.
4 Closets and doors Remove shelf dust, wipe handles, check corners, and leave closets empty and dry.
5 Floors Vacuum or sweep edges first, then mop compatible hard floors from the farthest room toward the exit.

Do a final inspection pass

  1. Open every cabinet, drawer, closet, and appliance compartment.
  2. Look at the apartment from the doorway and from floor level; dust collects where normal eye height misses it.
  3. Check window tracks, baseboards, light switches, door edges, and the space behind appliances.
  4. Remove your own adhesive, hooks, labels, shelf liner, and trash before taking the final photos.
  5. Record meter readings or key handoff details if your move-out instructions require them.

What to skip

  • Harsh chemicals everywhere: stronger is not automatically safer for paint, laminate, stone, or fixture finishes.
  • Scrubbing normal wear: do not sand, bleach, or abrade a surface just to make ordinary aging disappear.
  • New touch-up paint without a match: a visible color mismatch can look worse than a documented existing mark.
  • Large bulk packs: move-out is the wrong time to create leftovers you must transport or store.
  • Cleaning before documenting damage: take clear photos first, including wider room views and close-ups.

Deposit and move-out notes

Cleaning can improve the condition of an apartment, but it cannot guarantee a deposit outcome. Follow the lease and any written move-out checklist, distinguish your damage from pre-existing wear, and ask the landlord or property manager before making repairs that could change a surface. If you find a leak, mold, pest activity, broken appliance, or electrical issue, document it and report it instead of trying to hide it with cleaning products.

FAQ

What cleaning supplies do I need to move out of an apartment?

Start with microfiber cloths, a mop or floor tool, sponges, gloves, trash bags, and one targeted cleaner for residue or stubborn sink and kitchen marks. Add products only for surfaces your apartment actually has.

What should I clean first when moving out?

Take photos first, then clean empty cabinets and appliances, the kitchen, the bathroom, closets, and finally the floors. Working from high surfaces to low surfaces keeps you from re-dirtying finished areas.

Can cleaning products help me get my security deposit back?

They can help with removable dirt and residue, but they do not guarantee a deposit return. Follow the lease, document pre-existing damage, and ask before attempting repairs or aggressive stain removal.

Should I patch or paint before moving out?

Only if your lease or property manager instructs you to do so and you know the correct material and finish. An unapproved patch or mismatched paint can create more work.
