About

Apartment advice for people who cannot renovate.

Apartment Survival Kits is a practical library of minimal renter-friendly kits: what to buy, what to skip, and what might create damage, clutter, or move-out trouble.

The angle

Most apartment content starts with aesthetics. This site starts with constraints: small spaces, leases, low budgets, no drilling, shared laundry, bad lighting, move-out inspections, and products that need to be worth carrying to the next place.

The page standard

Each finished kit should explain the minimum useful setup, optional upgrades, what not to buy, renter risk, and move-out notes. Product links should support the recommendation, not replace it.

Editorial rules

What makes a kit worth publishing.

The site is built for renters who want fewer purchases and better decisions, not an endless list of nice-to-have apartment products.

It solves a real renter problem

A kit should start with a specific constraint: no pantry, no closet, no ceiling light, no drilling, musty closet, tiny bathroom, move-out cleaning, or first-night chaos.

It separates basics from upgrades

The minimum kit comes first. Optional upgrades are clearly marked so the page does not become a shopping list pretending to be advice.

It includes skip notes

Useful buying advice should tell you what to avoid: oversized organizers, weak lighting, risky adhesives, duplicate tools, and products that are annoying to move out.

How product links are handled

Some pages include Amazon affiliate links. If you click one and buy something, the site may earn a commission. The goal is still to make the page useful before the link: product picks should follow from the renter problem, the space constraint, and the minimum kit logic.

Current scope

The site is focused on apartment kits for move-in, kitchens, cleaning, no-drill fixes, small spaces, daily annoyances, and move-out prep. New pages are added when the topic is specific enough to be useful and broad enough that many renters may face it.
