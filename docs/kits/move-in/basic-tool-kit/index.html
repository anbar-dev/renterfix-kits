Quick verdict

If this is your first apartment, buy a compact household tool kit before you move. You do not need a 300-piece mechanics set. You need a hammer, tape measure, screwdrivers, pliers, hex keys, utility knife, level, and a small way to store everything so it does not disappear into a junk drawer.

What to skip until later

  • Huge mechanics sets: renters rarely need 200 sockets before they own a car project or garage.
  • Wall anchors on day one: read the lease first and know what kind of wall you are drilling.
  • Cheap drill bits for every surface: drywall, brick, tile, and plaster behave differently.
  • Stud finder before a plan: useful later, but not the first tool if you are avoiding holes.
  • Permanent hooks: try removable hooks and freestanding storage before making holes near the door.

Renter risk notes

Tools are safe. Tool decisions are where renters get expensive. A hammer, tape measure, and screwdriver do not risk your deposit. Drilling holes, overloading adhesive, stripping screws, or removing paint with sticky hooks can. The safest rule is simple: use manual tools for furniture and small fixes; use removable products for walls unless your lease clearly allows holes.

FAQ

What tools does every apartment renter need?

Most renters need a hammer, tape measure, screwdrivers, pliers, hex keys, utility knife, level, and a small storage bag or case. Add a drill only if you build furniture often or have permission for wall work.

Do renters need a drill?

Not always. A drill is useful for assembling furniture and some approved installs, but it is not required for a first apartment. If your lease restricts wall holes, treat the drill as a furniture tool, not a wall tool.

Are Command strips safe for apartment walls?

They are lower risk than nails or screws, but not risk-free. Surface type, paint quality, weight, humidity, and removal technique all matter.

Should I buy a cheap tool kit or individual tools?

For a first apartment, a compact kit is usually fine. Buy individual upgrades only after one tool annoys you enough to justify a better version.
