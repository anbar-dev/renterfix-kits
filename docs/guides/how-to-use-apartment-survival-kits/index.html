How to use the site

Start with the apartment problem, not the shopping list.

Apartment Survival Kits helps renters make smaller, better decisions: identify the constraint, choose the minimum useful fix, and wait before buying the next thing.

The shortest way to find the right page

Begin with the problem that is making the apartment harder to use. Do you need to clean before move-in, create storage without drilling, make a tiny kitchen work, or prepare for move-out? Choose a kit when the problem is specific. Choose a guide when you are comparing approaches or deciding what to skip.

The Apartment Survival Kits rule

Buy only what your rental actually needs. Every item should solve a real problem, fit the space, respect the lease, and have a clear removal plan.

Know when to open a kit or a guide

Open a kit

Use a kit when you can name one concrete constraint, such as no pantry, no ceiling light, a musty closet, or a narrow entryway. The page gives you a compact minimum setup, optional upgrades, skip notes, and renter-risk reminders.

Open a guide

Use a guide when the decision comes first: whether to buy, which mounting approach to compare, how to score deposit risk, or how to avoid overbuying for a first apartment.

Use the five-step renter workflow

  1. Name the constraint. Write down what is not working instead of starting with a product category.
  2. Measure the space. Check the doorway, wall, cabinet, floor, or storage area before buying anything that must fit.
  3. Buy the minimum. Choose the smallest useful solution that makes the room functional.
  4. Check renter risk. Review the lease and consider adhesive, moisture, weight, heat, installation, and removal.
  5. Upgrade after repetition. If the problem still happens after normal use, add one targeted improvement rather than a complete system.

What the README principle means in practice

The project is intentionally built around compact, practical pages instead of giant "everything you need" lists. That means you can skip:

  • Storage systems bought before the room is measured.
  • Duplicates of tools, appliances, or organizers that solve no current problem.
  • Permanent mounts or finishes before checking the lease.
  • Adhesive products that have not been tested on the actual surface.
  • Specialty cleaning products when a basic, material-safe option is enough.

"Renter-friendly" is not a guarantee of damage-free removal. Paint condition, surface type, humidity, weight, installation, and removal technique all matter. When a change could affect property, ask the landlord or maintenance team first.

FAQ

What is Apartment Survival Kits?

It is a practical renter resource for choosing compact kits and buying guides for move-in, daily apartment problems, small spaces, no-drill fixes, and move-out.

How do I know which page to read first?

Choose a kit when you have a specific apartment problem. Choose a guide when you are comparing solutions or deciding what not to buy.

Should I buy everything before moving in?

No. Buy basic function and safety items first, then wait to measure the apartment and observe your routine before adding storage, decor, duplicates, or upgrades.

Does renter-friendly mean damage-free?

No. Test products on a hidden spot, follow the instructions, check your lease, and ask before making a change that could affect the property.
