Renter decision tool

How deposit risk scores work.

A practical way to judge renter products before installation, based on the surface, load, moisture, effort, reversibility, and move-out consequences.

The score is a decision aid, not a guarantee

Apartment Survival Kits uses a 0/5 to 5/5 deposit risk score to make tradeoffs visible. A low score means the setup is generally easier to reverse under ordinary conditions. It does not promise that a product is damage-free, allowed by a lease, or suitable for every surface.

Start with the lowest-risk solution that solves the problem

Try a freestanding, tension-based, or otherwise reversible option before adding adhesive, weight, heat, moisture, drilling, or electrical changes to a rental.

The 0/5 to 5/5 scale

Score What it usually means Typical renter response
0/5 No attachment, no permanent change, and little consequence if the item is removed. Usually the easiest starting point, while still keeping walkways and outlets safe.
1/5 Lightweight and reversible, with a limited surface or floor interaction. Use normally after checking the surface and instructions.
2/5 Some pressure, adhesive, weight, or removal technique is involved. Test first, follow instructions, and keep the load conservative.
3/5 Failure or removal could mark paint, floors, fixtures, or another finished surface. Check the lease, test a hidden area, and consider a lower-risk alternative.
4/5 Heavy load, moisture, heat, permanent attachment, or complicated removal is involved. Ask the landlord or maintenance before proceeding.
5/5 The setup changes wiring, plumbing, structure, security hardware, or another building system. Do not treat it as a DIY renter upgrade. Get written permission or use the building's approved process.

What raises the risk score

The score reflects several factors together. One risky factor can matter more than several low-risk ones when the consequence of failure is large.

Surface

Smooth painted drywall, wallpaper, tile, wood, glass, metal, and textured surfaces behave differently. A product label cannot remove the need to check the actual surface.

Load

Count the item, the contents, and movement over time. A shelf that is light when empty can become a very different risk after it is filled.

Moisture and heat

Bathrooms, kitchens, windows, heaters, and direct sun can change adhesive performance, surface condition, and failure consequences.

Installation

Drilling, hardwiring, pressure, glue, cutting, and complicated assembly all increase the chance of a mistake or a difficult removal.

Reversibility

A product is lower risk when it can be removed cleanly without scraping, repainting, patching, disassembling a fixture, or leaving hardware behind.

Failure consequence

A falling frame is different from a falling shelf. A slippery mat is different from a loose handhold. Score the outcome, not just the installation method.

How the score changes by situation

Situation Starting risk Why it can change
Freestanding lamp Low Risk rises if cords cross walkways, outlets are overloaded, or the lamp is unstable.
Light frame on a compatible smooth wall Low to medium Risk rises with heavy frames, unknown paint, textured walls, humidity, or rushed removal.
Tension rod in a measured opening Low Risk rises when the sides are weak, slippery, damp, irregular, or overloaded.
Freestanding storage unit Low for walls Floor scratches, blocked exits, door clearance, and tipping can create other risks.
Drilled shelf or hardwired fixture High The attachment changes the wall or building system and may require permission and professional work.

How to score a product before buying

  1. Name the exact surface: do not score an abstract wall, floor, cabinet, or fixture.
  2. Estimate the real load: include contents, movement, and the possibility that the item will be used differently later.
  3. Check the environment: note moisture, heat, sunlight, dust, and cleaning products.
  4. Read the lease and instructions: permission and manufacturer limits are part of the risk calculation.
  5. Choose a fallback: know what you will use if the surface fails a test or the item does not fit.
  6. Plan removal before installation: take photos, keep instructions, and do not wait until move-out to learn the removal method.

What a low score does not mean

Do not convert a low score into a lease guarantee

Low deposit risk is not the same as permission, structural safety, electrical approval, or protection from every possible mark. Ask your landlord or maintenance when the setup touches building systems or the lease is unclear.

  • It does not mean an adhesive product works on every paint or wall finish.
  • It does not mean a pressure-fit item cannot slip or leave a mark.
  • It does not mean freestanding furniture cannot scratch floors or block access.
  • It does not mean a small repair should be attempted without knowing the building process.

FAQ

What does a 0/5 deposit risk score mean?

It usually means the setup does not attach to the apartment or make a permanent change, and removal has limited consequences. You still need to keep it stable and use it safely.

Are adhesive products always high deposit risk?

No. Risk depends on the product, surface, load, environment, installation, and removal. A light item on a compatible surface can be lower risk than a heavy item on an unsuitable one.

What renter products should have a high risk score?

Products involving drilling, hardwiring, plumbing changes, structural attachment, heavy overhead loads, or complicated removal should be treated as high risk and discussed with the landlord or maintenance.

Can a freestanding product still damage my apartment?

Yes. Furniture can scratch floors, tip, block doors, restrict vents, or create a moving problem. Low wall risk does not mean zero total risk.
